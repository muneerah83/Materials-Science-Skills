# Tier 2 — High-Impact Domain Journal Review Standard
# Target: Acta Materialia, JMCA, JACERS, Progress in Polymer Science, Composites Part A/B,
#         Nano Letters (lower end), Corrosion Science, Journal of Materials Science
# Scope: High-impact materials journals; quantitative mechanism and literature benchmark required
# ADDITIVE to Tier 1 — apply Tier 1 first, then layer these requirements.

## Novelty bar (stricter than Tier 1)

The gap must be specific and grounded in recent literature (≤5 years).
"First study of X" claims require a systematic literature search result or citation evidence,
not just an assertion. Reviewers will check recent issues of the journal itself.
Incremental improvement alone is insufficient — the work must close a named gap or
demonstrate a principle that generalizes beyond the tested system.

## Mechanism evidence (quantitative required)

Qualitative characterization is insufficient at this tier for mechanism claims:
- Kinetics: activation energy (Arrhenius plot) or reaction order for time-dependent processes
- Quantitative structure-property correlation: R² value or regression model linking
  characterization data (grain size, porosity, phase fraction) to measured properties
- For polymer systems: crosslink density from DMA plateau (not Tg alone)
- For ceramics: Weibull modulus and grain size quantified, not just described

Claims like "denser microstructure leads to higher strength" require quantified
pore volume fraction and its statistical correlation with strength.

## Statistics (beyond mean ± SD)

- ANOVA or t-test required when comparing ≥3 groups or claiming statistical significance
- Report effect size alongside p-value; p < 0.05 alone is not sufficient
- Pearson or Spearman R² required for structure-property correlation claims
- For DOE experiments: response surface or main effect analysis expected

## Comparison scope

Comparison with state-of-the-art from recent literature required alongside internal control.
"Better than baseline" is not enough — "better than or competitive with the best reported
for this system class" is the expected framing.

## Reproducibility signal

Multi-batch validation or inter-laboratory reproducibility strengthens the paper significantly.
Single-batch results without batch-to-batch verification are a weakness at this tier.

## Figure quality

- Publication-quality resolution (≥300 DPI for raster, SVG preferred)
- Consistent color scheme and axis formatting across all panels
- Statistical annotations on performance plots (significance brackets or letters)
- Mechanism diagrams must distinguish measured from inferred pathways

## Additional desk-reject risks at this tier (beyond Tier 1)

- Gap statement contradicted by recent literature in the same journal
- Mechanism from qualitative characterization only for a quantitative claim
- No state-of-the-art comparison — comparison vs. control only
- Single batch or single synthesis run without reproducibility check
- No formal statistical test for "significant improvement" claims
