# Tier 3 — Flagship Journal Review Standard
# Target: Nature Materials, ACS Nano, Advanced Materials, Advanced Functional Materials,
#         Nature Communications (Materials), Nano Letters (top end), Science Advances
# IF range: >15
# ADDITIVE to Tier 1 + Tier 2 — apply both first, then layer these requirements.

## The core question a Tier 3 reviewer asks

"Why is this Nature Materials and not Acta Materialia?"
If the answer is only "better performance numbers," that is not sufficient.
The finding must advance a principle, resolve a conceptual question, or demonstrate
something unexpected that reframes how the field thinks about the material or mechanism.

## Scientific significance (mandatory at this tier)

Every Tier 3 manuscript must have a clear answer to:
  "What can the field do or understand now that it could not before this work?"

Incremental performance improvement — even large improvements — is desk-reject risk
unless tied to a conceptual advance. Flag any manuscript that cannot answer this question.

## Unexpected or counterintuitive finding

Tier 3 papers are expected to contain at least one result that surprises.
If every result confirms what the authors predicted from existing theory, the work
is competent but not flagship-level. Reviewers look for:
  - A property relationship that inverts the expected trend
  - A mechanism operating at an unexpected scale or via an unexpected pathway
  - A material outperforming established benchmarks by a margin that demands explanation

## Generalizability (required, not optional)

The principle must extend beyond the single tested system:
  - Demonstrated across ≥2 material compositions or ≥2 processing conditions, OR
  - Supported by a theoretical model or simulation that predicts broader applicability, OR
  - Clearly articulated as a testable hypothesis for other systems

"Works for this specific formulation" is not generalizability.

## Mechanistic depth

First-principles (DFT, MD) support or atomic/molecular-level direct evidence is
strongly expected. At minimum, a quantitative model linking atomic/microstructural
features to the macroscopic property must be provided.
Phenomenological description ("denser microstructure → higher strength") is insufficient.

## Impact positioning

The manuscript must compare against the best reported values in the literature,
not just against an internal control. If the current best is X, the manuscript
should explain why this work matters even if X is not surpassed.
Reviewers will independently check the claim against the literature within 10 minutes.

## Statistical and reproducibility standard

- n ≥ 5 replicates for key claims (n ≥ 3 is not enough for flagship claims)
- Independent replication or multi-lab validation strongly preferred
- All raw data deposited in a public repository (FAIR data compliance expected)
- Statistical model selection justified (why ANOVA vs. mixed-effects vs. Bayesian)

## Desk-reject risk indicators unique to Tier 3

- Scientific significance not stated beyond "better performance"
- All results confirm existing theory — no unexpected finding
- Generalizability limited to one specific composition or condition
- Mechanism phenomenological only, no atomic/molecular evidence
- No comparison against state-of-the-art best-reported values
- Novelty claim contradicted by a recent Nature/ACS Nano paper (reviewer will find it)
- FAIR data not available or referenced
