## Domain-specific review axes

- **Percolation threshold curve**: "Percolation threshold" requires sigmoidal loading-property curve
  across multiple filler loadings. A single loading point cannot establish a threshold.
  Electrical percolation (conductivity vs. loading) or rheological percolation (G' vs. loading) required.
- **Quantitative dispersion metric**: "Well dispersed" must be supported by TEM/SEM image analysis
  with a quantitative metric (inter-particle spacing, agglomerate size distribution, area fraction).
  Low-magnification SEM or visual inspection is not acceptable.
- **Volume fraction vs. interface attribution**: Property improvement must distinguish between
  simple volume fraction effect (rule of mixtures) and genuine interface/interphase contribution.
  DMA constrained polymer fraction (Tg shift) provides interphase evidence.
- **Matrix integrity**: For barrier properties, void-free matrix and filler orientation must
  be verified by TEM cross-section, not assumed from processing conditions.

## Domain-specific fatal flaws

- Percolation threshold from single-loading conductivity data.
- "Well dispersed" from low-magnification SEM without quantitative dispersion analysis.
- Property improvement attributed to interface without separating volume fraction contribution.
- Barrier performance without TEM verification of filler orientation and void absence.

## Domain-specific reviewer questions

- "Is the loading-property curve sigmoidal with data points spanning below and above the threshold?"
- "Is dispersion quantified (inter-particle spacing, agglomerate area fraction) from TEM/SEM?"
- "Is the volume fraction contribution subtracted before claiming an interface effect?"
- "Is filler alignment and matrix integrity verified by TEM cross-section for barrier claims?"
