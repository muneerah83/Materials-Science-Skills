# Non-Ferrous Alloys (Al, Cu, Mg, Ti) — Reviewer Delta
# Adds to the metals-template. Load metals.md first, then this file.

## Domain-specific review axes

- **Age-hardening curve**: For precipitation-hardenable alloys (Al 2xxx/6xxx/7xxx, Cu-Be),
  hardness vs. aging time/temperature curve is required — not just peak-aged properties.
- **Anisotropy in wrought products**: Extruded Al or rolled Mg sheet must address orientation effects.
  Single-orientation tensile data misrepresents the material's structural performance.
- **Galvanic coupling**: In multi-material assemblies (Ti/Al, Al/steel), galvanic corrosion potential
  must be assessed. Corrosion of one alloy is accelerated in the presence of a nobler metal.
- **Density normalization**: Specific strength (strength/density) is more meaningful than absolute
  strength for lightweight alloy comparisons; reviewers expect both.

## Domain-specific fatal flaws

- Age-hardening optimization without full time-at-temperature curve.
- Wrought Al/Mg data from one orientation only — anisotropy uncharacterized.
- Galvanic effects ignored in dissimilar-metal joint applications.
- Absolute strength compared without density normalization for lightweight claims.

## Domain-specific reviewer questions

- "Is the full age-hardening curve (hardness vs. time) shown for peak-aged and overaged conditions?"
- "Are properties reported in more than one orientation for extruded/rolled products?"
- "Is galvanic coupling assessed when the alloy is used in contact with dissimilar metals?"
- "Is specific strength (strength/density) compared for lightweight design claims?"
